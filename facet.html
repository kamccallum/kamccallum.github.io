Instruction Manual Classification System

Quick Description

  • After acquiring a number of appliances and furniture pieces for my new home in Vancouver, I had accumulated a small collection of instruction manuals.

  • I decided to create a small, faceted classification system for this collection.

  • As you can see, I created 7 facets and 15 isolates overall. The bottom of this image is an example of how this classification would be applied to two of my manuals.


Learning Outcomes

If I were to do this assignment all over again, I would most likely change the subject of this classification system to be something that interests me more than instruction manuals. I was trying to be unique by organizing information-rich resources other than books, but my enjoyment suffered as a result.

In the original assignment, I attempted to apply the 5 Ranganathan fundamental categories. The facet for energy was “the primary type of instruction,” with operating, assembly, and installation being the isolates. However, I received peer feedback that a manual could be equally dedicated to two types of instruction, such as operating and assembly. Thus, I decided to create three facets instead of one that simply said whether the manual included these types of instructions. I also added units to the last facet, “length of manual,” to make it clear that these numerical ranges are the number of pages. Because of these changes, I took away the fundamental category labels, as I don’t think that this final product is very representative of what Ranganathan had in mind.
