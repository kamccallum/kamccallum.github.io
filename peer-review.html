CLASSIFICATION - CREATE

I love that you classified your books, as I feel that I can get to know you a little bit better this way. I think you did a great job choosing the facets themselves, as this is the information that I would wish to know about each book on my shelf. Also, nice job applying the Ranganathan fundamental facets! I only wonder if it would apply to your facets better if the genre facet was referring to personality. The Discipline of Organizing says that personality is the type of thing, which I would more closely associate with genre, but then I’m not sure where you would put the read status. I would also maybe clarify that read status is the facet and unread is the isolate. I think that you successfully maintained mutual exclusivity between genres, although you may eventually acquire a book outside of the categories listed, like a book on politics/contemporary affairs. For maintenance and revision purposes, I might also suggest a slight edit of the time facet so that the last isolate is 6+; this ensures that a revision isn’t necessary in a year’s time. Lastly, I wonder if perusing a book isn’t a type of partially reading? All in all, you did a great job at describing the essence of your resources!

Your system reminds me slightly of the example from our reading, which describes shirts, and is thus a great example of faceted classification. You seem to be describing quite a large collection, since the shoe size spans from toddler to big kid. Since the collection isn’t for an individual but rather many people, I might move the shoe size up in importance slightly—maybe below shoe type but above arch support. This way, arch support and width would be beside one another in the system, which feels more natural to me (although it may not to others). In terms of mutual exclusivity, do you think athletic shoes might be a type of casual shoe, since they are worn in a casual environment? Might “everyday shoe” be a more specific way to refer to a casual shoe that is not athletic? Lastly, and this is nitpicky, I might suggest clear distinctions when listing the attributes of a resource. It may be easier to read your system if the listed isolates were in order with commas between them and the size imitated the possible isolates. As it is right now, I’m not sure if the size of resource 1 is toddler or big kid, as 6 lies within both 4-10 and 4-7. However, listing the isolates of a resource wasn’t even necessary for this project, so it’s really not essential to your classification system! Like I mentioned before, this is a great example of facets—one which clearly indicates that you understand the fundamentals!

Wow—this is so cool! Your classification system is highly detailed, to the benefit of anyone who hopes to better understand their garden. I think that most gardeners could likely use your facets with only a slight change in the composition of isolates. That being said, I don’t know a lot about plants, and I’m not sure how broad the plant family isolates are. Do you think most gardeners could use your listed plant families if they were to implement this system for their own collection? Moreover, I wonder what your reasoning is for the order of facets? Is days to maturity the least important category because it has no bearing on how you care for the plants? All in all, I think this is an excellent system, which clearly shows your dedication to the resources themselves. Although they are neither information bearing nor categorized according to Ranganathan’s facets, the detail of your assignment goes well beyond the requirements. If I had any other suggestion to make, it would just be a slight edit to the last isolate in row spacing, so that it is “25 and over” rather than “over 25.” Great job!

CONTENT STANDARD - ANALYZE

I like that you chose to analyze an unconventional content standard, or one that is not associated with a library. Your critique reminds me of another assignment I read, in which a student was analyzing the tag system of the fanfiction database Archive of Our Own (Ao3). Similar to Ao3, it seems that Discogs relies on its users to provide information. As you pointed out, this may lead to incorrect or misleading metadata. However, a system created by users for users is certainly an empowering one, and the sheer amount of information you can collect is an advantage. I’d be interested to know what your ultimate assessment of Discog’s crowdsourcing nature is; do you believe it is helpful or a hindrance? Do you think the professional moderators are making a significant difference? Great job!

It’s really interesting how you analyzed the Etsy content standard from the buyer’s point of view. I like how you pointed out the invisible labor associated with optimizing a listing’s metadata. Do you think it would be better for Etsy sellers if the directions for each attribute were longer and included more information about optimization? You mentioned that there are articles about methods for filling out metadata, but I wonder if the content standard itself would improve if these recommendations were added, rather than forcing sellers to find it separately online. Overall, I very much enjoyed your assignment, and I think that this information would be useful to any user of Etsy, whether buyer or seller.
